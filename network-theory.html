1. Basic Concepts

Network Theory studies the behaviour of electrical circuits containing resistors, capacitors, inductors and sources.

Important terms

  • Node: A point connecting two or more circuit elements.
  • Branch: A path containing a circuit element or elements between nodes.
  • Loop: Any closed path in a circuit.
  • Mesh: A loop that contains no other loop inside it in a planar circuit.
  • Active element: An element capable of supplying energy, such as an ideal source.
  • Passive element: An element that absorbs or stores energy, such as R, L or C.
  • Linear circuit: A circuit whose response satisfies superposition.
GATE tip: A mesh is always a loop, but a loop is not necessarily a mesh.

2. Kirchhoff's Laws

Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)

The algebraic sum of currents at a node is zero.

Σ I = 0
Total current entering a node = Total current leaving the node.

Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)

The algebraic sum of voltages around a closed loop is zero.

Σ V = 0
Sum of voltage rises = Sum of voltage drops.

Ohm's Law

V = IR

Here V is voltage, I is current and R is resistance.

3. Important Formula Sheet

Resistance and Power

Series resistors: Req = R₁ + R₂ + ... + Rₙ
Parallel resistors: 1/Req = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ... + 1/Rₙ
Electrical power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R

Capacitor and Inductor

Capacitor: i = C(dv/dt)
Capacitor energy: W = ½CV²
Inductor: v = L(di/dt)
Inductor energy: W = ½LI²

Time Constants

RC circuit: τ = RthC
RL circuit: τ = L/Rth

For first-order circuits, the response reaches approximately 63.2% of its total change after one time constant during a rising exponential response.

AC Circuit Impedance

Resistor: ZR = R
Inductor: ZL = jωL
Capacitor: ZC = 1/(jωC)
Resonance (ideal series RLC): ω₀ = 1/√(LC)

4. Important Network Theorems

Superposition Theorem

In a linear circuit with multiple independent sources, the response is the algebraic sum of responses due to each independent source acting alone.

  • Deactivate an ideal independent voltage source by replacing it with a short circuit.
  • Deactivate an ideal independent current source by replacing it with an open circuit.
  • Keep dependent sources active.
  • Superposition applies to voltage and current, not directly to power.

Thevenin's Theorem

A linear two-terminal network can be represented by an equivalent voltage source Vth in series with resistance Rth (or impedance Zth in AC analysis).

Vth = Open-circuit terminal voltage
Rth = Equivalent resistance seen from the terminals

For independent sources only, deactivate the sources when finding Rth. With dependent sources, use a test source or another valid method.

Norton's Theorem

A linear two-terminal network can be represented by a current source IN in parallel with RN.

IN = Short-circuit terminal current
RN = Rth

Maximum Power Transfer

For a DC resistive network, maximum power is delivered to the load when the load resistance equals the Thevenin resistance.

RL = Rth
Pmax = Vth²/(4Rth)

For AC networks, maximum average power transfer occurs when the load impedance is the complex conjugate of the Thevenin impedance.

5. First-Order Transient Analysis

In a first-order circuit, the natural response decays exponentially. For a step response, the final value and initial value determine the response.

General form:
x(t) = x(∞) + [x(0⁺) − x(∞)]e−t/τ
  • Capacitor voltage cannot change instantaneously under finite current.
  • Inductor current cannot change instantaneously under finite voltage.
  • At DC steady state, an ideal capacitor behaves as an open circuit.
  • At DC steady state, an ideal inductor behaves as a short circuit.
Remember: vC(0⁺) = vC(0⁻) and iL(0⁺) = iL(0⁻), assuming no impulsive excitation.
